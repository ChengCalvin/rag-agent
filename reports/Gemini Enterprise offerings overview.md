# Gemini Enterprise rents you the platform, not enforcement

Gemini Enterprise, as of November 2026, is Google's employee-facing assistant and agent platform. It combines chat, permission-aware enterprise search, notebooks, Deep Research, a no-code Workflow Builder and an agent marketplace, sold in five editions: Business, Standard, Plus, Frontline and Pay-as-you-go ([editions doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/editions)). Google publishes no list prices in anything the research reached. The only figures, **Business from about $21 per seat per month and Standard/Plus "from $30"**, come from one unofficial third-party article ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/gemini-enterprise-pricing)). Its per-user authorization is real but sits in the index and identity layer. It uses source ACLs, `acl_info` metadata you supply, and workforce identities. It is not a code hook you control per query. Pro-code agents run on the separate Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform (formerly Vertex AI), where ADK agents hosted on Agent Runtime can be registered into the Gemini Enterprise app. For a small customer-support RAG agent with code-enforced access and a fixed five-agent pipeline, Gemini Enterprise is best treated as an optional front end and hosting route for the existing ADK build. It is a poor replacement for that build. The reasons are an employee-identity model, a free-key constraint that does not carry over, and several preview or unconfirmed features.

## Five editions, but only one third-party price list

The editions doc lists Business (1-500 users, 25 GiB pooled storage per user), Standard (1+ users, 30 GiB), Plus (1+ users, 75 GiB), Frontline (needs 150+ users of Standard or Plus, 2 GiB) and Pay-as-you-go (one seat to start) ([editions doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/editions)). Google's support page positions Business for small teams without complex IT, Standard for employees in large organizations, Plus for those needing the most agent-building tooling, and Frontline as the low-cost tier ([Google support](https://support.google.com/g/answer/16547364?hl=en)). Business is a web SaaS app with no Cloud console or project-level IAM, and Frontline users cannot create skills or use anything but admin-provisioned agents ([editions doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/editions)).

Pricing is where the evidence is thinnest. Google's pricing page returned a 404 and the product page fetched empty, so the only price evidence is Dupple's report of Google's US page on 28 October 2026: Business from $21 self-serve with a trial, Standard from $30 via sales, and Plus shown with Standard rather than priced separately ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/gemini-enterprise-pricing)). Dupple also says Business is capped at 300 seats, while Google's own docs say 500; the Google figure should win. No source reached gives a Frontline or Plus price, trial length, or a perpetual free tier. Pay-as-you-go reached GA on 1 August 2026, opened to all invoiced billing accounts on 10 September, and gained prepaid credits for self-serve accounts on 5 November, but its per-unit rates were not found ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/release-notes)). Licensed editions can also enable "Pay-as-you-go usage above quota" with spend limits, and Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 are billed as consumption against the project cap ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/release-notes)).

On components, Workflow Builder (formerly Agent Designer) reached GA on 3 September 2026 and can import existing A2A and ADK agents, with the canvas assistant GA on 24 July and Projects GA on 4 September ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/release-notes)). Antigravity developer tools reached GA on 18 August, and new Standard and Plus subscriptions no longer include Gemini Code Assist, with existing entitlements ending 31 March 2027 ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/release-notes)). The app offers Gemini 4 models plus Claude Opus 5.5 and Sonnet 5.5 ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/release-notes)). Third-party sources attribute higher limits, custom agents, VPC Service Controls, CMEK and residency controls to Standard and Plus ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/gemini-enterprise-pricing)), but the per-edition feature matrix did not render in the fetch, so the edition-to-feature mapping is unverified. The release-notes summary also covered only mid-July to mid-November 2026.

## Connectors reach many systems, but custom data is the route that matters

The connector catalogue is broad. GA sources include Microsoft 365, SharePoint, OneDrive, Teams, ServiceNow, Jira, Confluence, Box, Slack, GitHub, Google Drive and Gmail, and Stripe. Salesforce, Zendesk, Dynamics 365, Notion, Shopify and a **Custom MCP Server connector** are public preview ([connector list](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/connect-third-party-data-source)). On-premises Jira and Confluence Data Center with ingestion are private preview, behind an allowlist ([same page](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/connect-third-party-data-source)). Cloud Storage and BigQuery serve as custom data stores, unstructured (PDF, HTML, DOCX, PPTX, XLSX) or structured ([intro to connectors](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/introduction-to-connectors-and-data-stores)). Connectors work in one of two modes. Federation queries the source live and copies nothing, though Google warns search quality may be lower. Ingestion copies content into the Gemini Enterprise index, which can improve quality ([intro to connectors](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/introduction-to-connectors-and-data-stores)). Federated queries, including parts of conversation history, go to the third party under that vendor's terms ([same page](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/introduction-to-connectors-and-data-stores)). Sync is incremental every three hours by default, but incremental sync captures neither identity data nor deletions, so revoked access depends on the full-sync cadence ([same page](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/introduction-to-connectors-and-data-stores)).

The Custom MCP connector has documented limits. It supports StreamableHTTP only, needs HTTPS with a publicly trusted certificate unless routed through Agent Gateway, leaves all actions off by default, and requires user confirmation on each call unless a tool sets `readOnlyHint` ([custom MCP setup](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/connectors/custom-mcp-server/set-up-custom-mcp-server)). The cap on enabled actions is 100 in Google's page; a Medium guide says 150, and the discrepancy is unresolved. Per-connector federation, edition and ACL-fidelity details were not on the list page, and the availability of website data stores in Gemini Enterprise was not verified.

## Authorization is enforced by the platform, not by your code

The enforcement model is stated plainly: users see and can have answers grounded only on what they can already access in the source application, and permissions are managed at the source, not in connector settings ([identity provider doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/configure-identity-provider)). Identity comes from Google Identity or a third-party OIDC/SAML provider (Entra ID, Okta, AD FS) through Workforce Identity Federation, and Microsoft 365 ingestion specifically requires Entra ID ([identity provider doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/configure-identity-provider)). Access control must be switched on when a data store is created and cannot be toggled afterwards. Each document admits at most 3,000 readers. Changing identity provider means deleting and recreating ingestion stores ([identity provider doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/configure-identity-provider)). Custom data carries `acl_info.readers[].principals[]` with user or group IDs, enabled by `aclEnabled` at store creation, and Google resolves the searching user through the identity provider ([custom-data access control](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/identity)). Entra ID users in more than 150 SAML groups or 200 OIDC groups lose group claims and hit 403 errors ([identity provider doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/configure-identity-provider)).

"Code-enforced" access therefore exists in two narrower forms. First, you supply ACL metadata at import and map identities, and the index filters on it. No documented mechanism lets you inject arbitrary per-query filter logic. Second, tool-side checks work inside agents you host. ADK agents registered in the app receive the user's email, and a per-user OAuth flow through an authorization resource can deliver a token to tools, with Custom MCP servers receiving the user's OAuth token in the `Authorization` header ([register ADK agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/register-and-manage-an-adk-agent); [custom MCP setup](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/connectors/custom-mcp-server/set-up-custom-mcp-server)). Several mechanics are undocumented: how a programmatic Search or Answer call carries user identity for ACL filtering, whether ACL principals can be arbitrary application IDs outside the identity provider, and how ADK on Agent Runtime exposes the email and token (state keys, session `user_id`). Community threads suggest the token sits in session state under the authorization ID, but that rests on forum snippets, and one thread reports the consent popup failing on a deployed agent. A further limit is the user population. This is an employee product with workforce identities, so a design where end customers authenticate and see only their own orders does not fit per-document ACLs, because the reader cap and identity model do not match.

## Agent building runs through ADK, A2A and MCP, with preview caveats

Gemini Enterprise lists Workflow Builder agents, prebuilt agents, custom ADK agents, A2A agents and Dialogflow agents ([agents overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/agents-overview)). After the April 2026 rebrand, Vertex AI became the Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, Agentspace was absorbed, and Agent Engine became Agent Runtime ([Google Cloud blog](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/introducing-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform); [TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-cloud-next-ai-agents-agentic-era)). Admins register an ADK agent hosted on Agent Runtime through the console or REST (the examples use `v1alpha`), which requires the Gemini Enterprise Admin role, an existing app and a deployed agent. Registration consumes a daily quota of 1 per user for Standard, 10 for Plus and 0 for Frontline ([register ADK agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/register-and-manage-an-adk-agent)). Custom A2A registration is labelled **Preview**, ignores agent-card security schemes, and cannot carry static API keys or custom headers ([register A2A agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/register-and-manage-an-a2a-agent)). Separately, A2A registration went GA on 17 August and three-legged OAuth for ADK A2A agents on Agent Runtime on 13 November ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/release-notes)). This conflicts with the per-page Preview label, and the Agent Starter Pack says A2A agents on Agent Engine are not yet supported for its register command ([starter pack](https://googlecloudplatform.github.io/agent-starter-pack/cli/register_gemini_enterprise.html)). The GA versus Preview status of A2A, ADK registration, the Custom MCP connector and Agent Identity Auth Manager is unresolved, because the pages involved do not state it consistently.

For topology, a registered ADK agent is the deployed root agent, so Sequential and Loop agents and callbacks should run as written. Google documents only performance cautions, such as limiting sequential sub-agent hops, setting `min_instances`, pinning package versions and bounding retries ([register ADK agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/register-and-manage-an-adk-agent)). No official example of a Sequential-plus-Loop pipeline was found, nor any statement on whether intermediate agent events appear in the UI or what the chat-turn timeout is. The no-code Workflow Builder is a weaker substitute. It has Gemini Agent steps (Gemini 3.0+ only), Condition steps with AND/OR, human "Request info" steps and MCP steps, and write actions need human review ([workflow agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/workflow-builder/workflow-agents)). The overview mentions loops but the step table lists none, and no code step is documented, so a retrieval-validator loop with a code-computed confidence score would need an MCP or ADK tool.

Agent Runtime pricing reportedly follows unified SKUs of **$0.085 per vCPU-hour, $0.009 per GiB-hour and $0.30 per GiB-month of storage**, with Sessions and Memory Bank metering from 1 September 2026 ([pricing PDF](https://services.google.com/fh/files/emails/b_502770571_gemini_enterprise_agent_platform_pricing.pdf); [ecorpit](https://ecorpit.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-cost-breakdown-2026/)). Both figures came from search snippets and a secondary summary, the free tier is unknown, and Gemini inference is billed separately. Agent Runtime does not accept a free AI Studio key, which is incompatible with the project's pinned stack.

## Guardrails and governance help but do not replace tool-side checks

Google says Gemini Enterprise does not use prompts, responses or connected data to train its foundation models ([security overview](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/security-overview)). VPC Service Controls apply to Standard and Plus but not when Grounding with Google Search is enabled. CMEK and Access Transparency are unavailable in the `global` region, and CMEK covers US and EU multi-regions only ([compliance controls](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/compliance-security-controls)). Listed certifications include HIPAA, FedRAMP, ISO 27001 and SOC 1/2/3, though Antigravity in Gemini Enterprise lacks several of them ([compliance controls](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/compliance-security-controls)). Data Access audit logs record `Search` calls, and an opt-in usage audit log captures prompts, responses and grounding metadata. One source names `agentspaceAdmin` as the needed role and another `discoveryengine.admin`, which stays unresolved ([audit logging](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/audit-logging)). One page claims default CMEK while the compliance and intro pages call it optional, and the latter two are the better-supported reading.

Model Armor is not automatic. Console settings do not protect registered ADK or A2A agents, so developers must call it through REST in agent code ([register ADK agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/register-and-manage-an-adk-agent)). Agent Gateway screening covers only ADK `streamQuery`, and A2A traffic does not pass through Agent Gateway ([register A2A agents](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/register-and-manage-an-a2a-agent)). Agent evaluations reached GA on 31 July 2026 ([Google Developers Blog](https://developers.googleblog.com/agent-and-model-evaluations-in-gemini-enterprise-agent-platform-are-now-ga/)), and observability is reported GA with Cloud Trace and Logging collected by default for ADK deployments ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/release-notes)). For retrieval, the Discovery Engine API exposes citation inclusion and a check-grounding threshold (default 0.6). No official documentation of hybrid retrieval, reranking or tunable thresholds in the Gemini Enterprise app itself was found ([check grounding](https://docs.cloud.google.com/generative-ai-app-builder/docs/check-grounding)).

## Residency and other unverified items

Data residency options listed are `us`, `eu` and `global` ([editions doc](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/editions)). **Canadian data residency is not confirmed anywhere in the research.** The only Canada-specific datum is that Gemini 2.5 Pro was discontinued in Canada and Japan on 20 October ([release notes](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini/enterprise/docs/release-notes)). Other unresolved items are Frontline and Plus prices, Business trial length, Agent Runtime free tier, Deep Research and Data Insights availability per edition, whether "Agent Gallery" equals "Agent Marketplace", the status and loop support of Workflow Builder's Agent Studio sibling, and the default state of Data Access logs. Several docs were read through a small-model summary or search snippets, so wording is paraphrased.

## Conclusion

The comparison is less a build-versus-buy choice than a question of where each layer lives. Gemini Enterprise's strongest offer, permission-aware search over connected enterprise sources, depends on workforce identities and platform-held ACLs, which fit an internal support team browsing documents far better than an agent that checks whether a given customer owns a given order. The project's tools (`search_policy`, `get_order_status`, `check_return_eligibility`) and its code-enforced guard are what remain valuable either way. Google's own architecture sends per-user decisions back to your tool code via the email, OAuth token or custom MCP backend, and Model Armor explicitly does not do tool-side authorization. The most defensible plan keeps the ADK pipeline, including the fixed five-agent flow and validator loop, and treats registration in Gemini Enterprise as a later, optional distribution step. That step would be worth pursuing only if employees need the agent inside a managed chat surface.

Three costs would arrive with that step. Hosting needs a billed Google Cloud project and Agent Runtime, which breaks the free AI Studio key constraint. Registration needs a paid seat or Pay-as-you-go app. And the hand-rolled numpy retrieval would need packaging into a container rather than being swapped for Gemini Enterprise search, which the architecture rules out. Before committing, verify four things with Google or in a trial: official per-seat prices, the GA status of the registration and MCP features, how the user identity reaches an ADK agent on Agent Runtime, and whether the five-agent pipeline's intermediate steps and latency behave acceptably in the chat UI.
